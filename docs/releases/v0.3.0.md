# Brimline 0.3.0

Brimline 0.3.0 hides the icons of the other apps with macOS's own allow-list of the menu bar on macOS 27 and later, and a click on Brimline's bar now hides and shows them. With this version, the repository and the site are public.

## What is new

- **On macOS 27, the icons go toward the bar.** When macOS has its allow-list of the menu bar (the one of its exam mode), Brimline hides with it instead of making its bar long. The icons of the apps left of the bar disappear in place, one app after the other toward the bar, and the bar becomes an arrow «; macOS draws no « of its own. A click on the arrow brings them back, from the nearest to the farthest. It works per app: an app with an icon on each side of the bar stays shown, and the items of the system (clock, Wi-Fi, Control Center) always stay. On macOS 26 and before, the bar still grows long to push the icons out of sight.
- **A click on the bar hides the icons**, like the "Hide the icons" buttons of the window and of Settings. If Brimline refuses to hide, Settings opens on Menu Bar, where the reason is written.
- **The Accessibility permission, on macOS 27.** Brimline uses it only to read where the icons of the other apps are, to know which are left of the bar; macOS asks for it the first time. Without it, Brimline refuses to hide and says what to turn on.
- **Brimline must be in `/Applications` to hide** on macOS 27: anywhere else, macOS hides its icons too. Otherwise Brimline refuses and says to move it. `scripts/install.sh` now installs there (or in `~/Applications` when `/Applications` is not writable) and moves a copy left in `~/Applications` by an earlier install to the Trash. The installer of the site already chose `/Applications`; it keeps a copy where it already is.
- **Quitting Brimline or turning the setting off shows every icon again.**
- **The menu bar window keeps the height of its content.** After a note went away, the panel no longer sits in a taller window with an empty band above it and a dark band under the "Quit" line.

## Good to know

- The allow-list is a private API of macOS that Apple does not document (Hidden Bar 1.11.1 uses it too): a later macOS can remove it, and Brimline then goes back to the length of its bar. It was tried on macOS 27.0.1 on a 1920 pt screen without a notch. See [docs/menu-bar.md](../menu-bar.md).
- Brimline is signed ad hoc, so macOS ties the Accessibility permission to one build. After an update, until the permission is given again, Brimline hides with the list of apps it found right of the bar the last time, which hides all the other apps at once, and it asks macOS to show its permission window (once per launch).
- Brimline is not notarized by Apple. A disk image downloaded in a browser needs **Open Anyway** once, in System Settings, Privacy & Security ([Apple's page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102445)).
